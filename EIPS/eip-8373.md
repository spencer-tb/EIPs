## Abstract

This document defines an incubation process for EIPs under active
prototyping ahead of network upgrade planning. Incubation attaches to the
EIP, persists across network upgrades, and composes with the per-upgrade
inclusion stages defined in EIP-7723. It lets the relevant layer's specification and testing
repositories publish executable specifications, test fixtures, benchmarks,
and devnets for an EIP before an upgrade meta-EIP exists. It confers no
inclusion status and no entitlement to repository resources.

## Motivation

EIP-7723 expects an executable specification with tests when an EIP is
Considered for Inclusion, and mandates them when an EIP is Scheduled for
Inclusion. Its stages, however, are scoped to a single upgrade and reset
between upgrades: there is no defined home for the engineering work that
produces this evidence before an upgrade's meta-EIP exists. In practice such
work happens anyway, on personal branches and one-off devnets, and
inclusion decisions may be made before its results are visible.

Large EIPs that may become future upgrade headliners often require
substantial specification, implementation, testing, benchmarking, and
interoperability work before their feasibility is understood. Waiting for an
upgrade's planning process to begin leaves too little time to perform this
work and creates a circular dependency: selecting a headliner requires
engineering evidence, but producing that evidence traditionally follows
selection.

Incubation breaks this dependency. It allows the heavy lifting for potential
headliners to begin while the current upgrade is being delivered, without
assigning the EIP to a future upgrade. When headliner selection begins,
client teams can evaluate working implementations and test results rather
than proposals alone, and the selected feature does not begin engineering
from zero.

The consensus layer resolves part of this problem with in-tree feature
specifications that can ship alongside scheduled forks without implying
scheduling. The execution layer has no equivalent shared designation.
Incubation defines one for both layers.

## Specification

The key words "MUST", "MUST NOT", "SHOULD", "SHOULD NOT", and "MAY" in this
document are to be interpreted as described in RFC 2119 and RFC 8174.

### Terminology and scope

"Incubating" and "Parked" are designations in the incubation register. They
are not EIP lifecycle statuses defined by EIP-1 and are not network-upgrade
inclusion stages defined by EIP-7723.

Incubation is available to Standards Track Core EIPs whose implementation
requires coordinated work by execution-layer or consensus-layer mainnet
client teams. An Incubating EIP may be a candidate future headliner or
supporting work for one.

Incubation is tracked separately for the execution and consensus layers. An
EIP affecting both layers enters, maintains, and exits incubation on each
layer independently.

Incubation MUST NOT be interpreted as:

* a commitment or preference to include the EIP in a network upgrade
* a Proposed, Considered, or Scheduled for Inclusion stage
* a guarantee that an artifact will be merged or released by a specification,
  testing, client, or infrastructure repository
* a commitment of maintainer, continuous-integration, audit, or devnet
  capacity.

Inclusion in any upgrade remains exclusively governed by the separate
per-upgrade process defined in EIP-7723 and its corresponding AllCoreDevs
Execution (ACDE) or AllCoreDevs Consensus (ACDC) decisions.

### Entering incubation

An EIP MAY enter incubation for a layer when all of the following hold:

* The EIP exists, is not Stagnant or Withdrawn, and has a named champion and
  a named artifact owner. One person MAY hold both roles.
* A public executable specification and normative test seed have been
  submitted as a branch or pull request against a declared baseline of the
  relevant specification or testing repository. The designated tests pass
  in public continuous integration.
* More than 40% of the layer's mainnet client teams are actively working on
  the EIP. A client team counts as actively working only when it has
  publicly linked substantial implementation code that exercises a material
  part of the EIP's proposed protocol behavior. Issues, implementation
  plans, expressions of interest, or engineer assignments alone do not
  satisfy the threshold. The set of mainnet client teams is the set
  AllCoreDevs Testing (ACDT) treats as the layer's active mainnet clients.
* The champion has opened a public incubation request linking the evidence
  above ahead of the ACDT call at which acknowledgment is requested.

Performance-sensitive proposals SHOULD also provide a reproducible
benchmark methodology and baseline.

ACDT acknowledges incubation when the criteria above are met and there is
rough consensus that the work is suitable for the incubation tier. Meeting
the criteria establishes eligibility, not automatic admission. An
objection MUST identify a failed entry criterion or a concrete technical,
security, repository-capacity, or process risk. The desirability of the
proposal for any network upgrade is out of scope for an objection. The
call facilitator MUST record the acknowledgment, each objection, and its
disposition. An objection is input to rough consensus and is not an
individual veto.

ACDT acknowledgment is an engineering coordination decision only. It does
not require ratification by ACDE or ACDC and MUST NOT determine an EIP-7723
stage. ACDE and ACDC retain exclusive authority over network-upgrade
inclusion decisions.

Following acknowledgment, the register MUST be updated by pull request. If a
champion or artifact owner is also a maintainer of a hosting repository,
they MUST disclose that role and MUST NOT be the sole approver of the
artifact merge.

### While Incubating

The layer's specification and testing repositories, including
execution-specs for the execution layer and consensus-specs for the
consensus layer, MAY publish executable specifications, test fixture
releases, and devnet configurations for Incubating EIPs. Each repository
defines how incubation artifacts are organized, reviewed, maintained, and
released, and retains authority over what it accepts, maintains, and
releases. ACDT acknowledgment cannot compel a repository to
merge or release an artifact.

Artifact names, manifests, and release notes MUST identify the EIP and label
the artifacts as experimental incubation-tier material. Incubation artifacts
MUST be isolated from mainnet and scheduled-upgrade artifacts so downstream
consumers cannot mistake them for canonical or deployment-ready outputs.
Each artifact set MUST identify its EIP revision, repository baseline,
known incompatibilities with other incubating work, and last passing
continuous-integration run.

Evidence produced during incubation MAY be considered when evaluating the
EIP under EIP-7723. Headliner candidates SHOULD normally demonstrate
public executable specifications, tests, and multi-client implementation
evidence before selection. Incubation is one means of producing and
maintaining this evidence. Lack of incubation MUST NOT prevent proposal or
selection, and incubation MUST NOT be interpreted as an inclusion
preference.

### Parked and removed

ACDT MAY move an EIP to Parked for a layer when it becomes Stagnant, its
champion or artifact owner is no longer active, the active-client threshold
is no longer
satisfied, its executable specification and tests fall behind the current
EIP revision or the latest development fork of the relevant repository, or
its designated tests no longer pass in public continuous integration. A
temporary lapse, such as a client team reallocating engineers to the
current upgrade, does not require Parking. Parked artifacts MUST
be retained in a publicly accessible form but MUST be excluded from new
releases and active devnet configurations. Repositories MAY move them out of
active development branches.

A Parked EIP returns to Incubating after ACDT verifies that these
requirements are restored and the register is updated. A new incubation
request is required only when the EIP has been Parked for more than twelve
months or a substantive objection remains unresolved.

An EIP is removed from the register for a layer when it is Withdrawn or
superseded, has been Parked for twelve months without an active champion,
or is removed
by rough consensus on ACDT. When a Core EIP reaches Final because it has been
deployed, its incubation entry SHOULD be replaced by a link to the upgrade in
which it was Included.

Removal from incubation does not alter the EIP's EIP-1 status and does not
prevent a later incubation request, except where EIP-1 makes the underlying
EIP terminal.

## Rationale

### Why not a new EIP-7723 stage?

The EIP-7723 stages live in a specific upgrade's meta-EIP. A stage for a
future upgrade has no meta-EIP to live in, and an EIP under incubation may
target an upgrade two or three forks out whose composition is unknown. A
per-upgrade stage would also read as a rung on the inclusion ladder, which is
precisely the signal incubation must not carry.

### Why does ACDT acknowledge incubation?

ACDT does not judge whether an EIP is desirable. It acknowledges that a
defined set of engineering conditions exists and coordinates shared
engineering work around it. Incubation evaluates active implementations,
executable specifications, tests, benchmarks, and devnets, and ACDT is the
venue where the people producing and consuming that evidence coordinate
their work. Requiring ACDE or ACDC
ratification would blur incubation with roadmap selection and create the
inclusion signal this designation is intended to avoid. Repository
maintainers independently retain control of repository resources, while
ACDE and ACDC retain control of upgrade inclusion.

### Why a client-activity threshold?

Sustained engineering work from a large minority of a layer's client teams
is revealed preference: it demonstrates that the proposal can produce useful
cross-client evidence without purporting to measure consensus about
inclusion. The threshold is a fraction rather than a fixed count so its
meaning can survive changes to the client set and differences between
layers. Public evidence prevents dormant or nominal implementations from
satisfying it indefinitely. Incubation recognizes demonstrated
multi-client R&D rather than bootstrapping interest in a proposal.

### Why can maintainers champion an EIP?

Repository maintainers are often among the people best equipped to develop
executable specifications and tests. Excluding them would remove useful
expertise without preventing informal influence. Disclosure, independent
approval, and repository discretion address the conflict more directly.

### Why is incubation per-layer?

Client sets, specification repositories, and coordination needs differ
between the execution and consensus layers, and a cross-layer EIP's
readiness can differ substantially between them.

### Why incubate before upgrade selection?

Incubation is intended to produce engineering evidence, not to advance an
EIP toward inclusion. Prototyping may demonstrate that an EIP is
impractical, requires redesign, or should not proceed. Parking, redesign,
or abandonment following incubation is therefore an expected outcome and
does not represent failure of the process.

### Why can an EIP be Parked?

The historical objection to off-schedule specification work is maintenance
cost against a moving base. The Parked designation retains useful work
without presenting stale artifacts as actively supported.

### Why does the register live in this EIP?

Upgrade meta-EIPs already track their inclusion lists in-document. Keeping
the register here follows the same pattern and avoids a normative
dependency on any external repository.

### A worked example

Purely illustrative, using the partitioned binary tree (EIP-8297), which
develops and modifies the unified binary tree design introduced by EIP-7864:

1. The EIP champion opens an incubation request containing an executable
   specification, a normative test seed, and public work from more than 40%
   of execution-layer mainnet client teams.
2. ACDT reviews the technical evidence and acknowledges execution-layer
   incubation by rough consensus. No ACDE inclusion decision is implied or
   required.
3. The register records the entry. execution-specs may publish isolated
   releases labeled as incubation-tier artifacts at its maintainers'
   discretion.
4. The owners periodically refresh the evidence, EIP revision, baseline, and
   passing tests. The results remain available to later headliner and
   inclusion discussions without granting the EIP inclusion preference.
5. If client activity, ownership, or artifact freshness lapses, the entry
   becomes Parked and its artifacts leave active releases. It returns after
   ACDT verifies that the requirements are again satisfied.

## Backwards Compatibility

This EIP does not alter the Ethereum protocol. It documents a coordination
practice and composes with, without modifying, the stages defined in
EIP-7723 or the lifecycle statuses defined in EIP-1.

## Security Considerations

Incubation gives experimental work greater visibility and can therefore be
mistaken for technical endorsement or an inclusion commitment. Required
labels, artifact isolation, decision records, and the prohibition on using
incubation as an inclusion prerequisite reduce this risk but cannot eliminate
social signaling.

Ambiguous client counting or unverifiable implementation claims could permit
governance capture. Publicly linked evidence and layer-specific
acknowledgment make such claims auditable.

Incubating many proposals can exhaust maintainer, continuous-integration,
release, and devnet capacity. Continued repository discretion permits
maintainers to reject, isolate, deprioritize, or Park work that cannot be
supported safely.

Implementations and tests may share an incorrect assumption, and passing
incubation tests is not evidence of production safety. Incubation artifacts
remain experimental and do not bypass the independent review, multi-client
interoperability, audit, and upgrade-inclusion requirements normally applied
before mainnet deployment.

Public prototype artifacts can also expose denial-of-service or consensus
failure techniques before mitigations are ready. Repository security and
responsible-disclosure policies continue to apply. Incubation does not
require premature public disclosure of an embargoed vulnerability.
